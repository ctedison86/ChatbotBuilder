Reading-Discussion Chatbot Builder

A chatbot is an AI program that lets students discuss a reading with it and test their ideas, interpretations, and questions. It responds with questions that push them to explain, support, and reconsider their reasoning. In about 10–15 minutes, you'll set one up for your course so that it prompts students to think for themselves rather than handing them answers. No AI experience needed.

About 10–15 minutes

What you'll need

  • Your reading(s) as a file or a link. Have more than one if you want students to compare texts.
  • Your UO login for Microsoft Copilot (your UO email and password).
  • Your Canvas course, where you'll add the link to your finished chatbot.

New to AI? anytime, from any screen.

Build a prompt for your reading-discussion chatbot

This page asks you a few simple questions, then turns your answers into one finished piece of text called a "prompt" — instructions that tell an AI tool how to act. When you're done, you'll paste that text into an AI tool to create your chatbot.

This page doesn't save anything — if you close it partway through, you'll need to start over, so once your prompt is finished, copy it somewhere safe (like a document on your computer).

This page is built around Microsoft Copilot. UO has an agreement that gives every faculty member and student free, secure access at copilot.cloud.microsoft — that's why it's the recommended path. Either way, the text you build here will work anywhere: you can also paste it into Claude, ChatGPT, or another AI website instead.
Have your reading ready before you finish this process. Near the end, you'll give your chatbot the actual reading — as a file or a link — directly in your AI platform. That's what lets the chatbot discuss your specific reading and sources with students, so it's worth keeping them nearby. You don't need them yet, though — feel free to start building your prompt below.

Follow these steps

Complete these steps now, in Microsoft Copilot, to turn your prompt into a working chatbot and get it in front of students.

  1. Go to copilot.cloud.microsoft and sign in with your UO email and password. Look for the green shield icon — that confirms you're in the protected, UO-approved version. Open Agent Builder and create a new agent.
  2. Paste your prompt into the agent's instructions. Then, on that same setup screen, scroll to the "Knowledge" section. If a reading is a file, click "Upload" (or just drag it in); if it's a web page or online article, click "Enter URL" and paste the link there instead. You can add more than one — mix files and links freely if you're using several texts. This is what actually gives your chatbot the full reading(s) to work with — more on why below.
  3. Share the agent with "Specific people in your organization," then copy its link and add that link into Canvas (for example, as a module item) so students can reach it.
  1. Go to claude.ai or chatgpt.com. Start a "Project" (Claude) or "Custom GPT" (ChatGPT) if you can — it saves your instructions so you don't retype them. Paste your prompt there.
  2. If it has a "Knowledge" or "Add content" option, upload your reading(s) there too, or paste in a link if it accepts one — most tools let you add more than one file or link if you're using several texts. More on why below. These tools aren't covered by UO's Copilot agreement, so check with your instructional designer or campus IT before using one with students, especially around student data privacy.
  3. If the tool lets you share a link to your chatbot, copy it and add that link into Canvas (for example, as a module item). Sharing options vary by platform, and students may need their own account.

Why attach the reading(s), too? The prompt text alone can't include your full reading(s) or a live link to them — attaching the actual file(s), or entering their URL(s), as "knowledge sources" gives your chatbot everything it needs to work with.

Not sure which to use? If your campus hasn't pointed you toward something else, Microsoft Copilot is the safest default — it's free for every UO student and already approved for this kind of use. Ask your instructional designer or IT support if you're unsure.
